Module 1 · Why microbiology matters

Sterility cannot be tested into a product.

It must be built in — through contamination control.
  • The sterility test is statistically limited — it only detects contamination above a threshold
  • Aseptic processing depends entirely on prevention, not detection
  • APS is the mechanism by which that prevention is proven
Key message You are the most critical contamination control measure in the cleanroom.
Module 1 · Relative risk

Two routes to a sterile product

Terminal sterilization

The product is sterilized in its final container. Provided bioburden is controlled, the risk of an unsterile unit is very low.

  • Robust, forgiving of minor process drift
  • Sterility assured by the lethal process

Aseptic processing

All components are sterilized separately and assembled aseptically. Even if everything is sterile, poor technique can contaminate the product.

  • The more manual the process, the higher the risk
  • Sterility assured only by operator discipline
Human error and poor aseptic technique are the top root causes of contamination.

Module 3 · Contamination control strategy

The sources of contamination

Personnel

Skin flakes, respiratory droplets, hair, jewellery, cosmetics, poor gowning

Air / HVAC

Turbulence from rapid movement, door openings, loss of first-air protection

Surfaces & equipment

Contact with non-sterile surfaces, inadequate disinfection, condensation

Materials

Damaged primary packaging, compromised sterile barriers

Water / moisture

Cleaning residues, spills, condensation in isolators and RABS

Human behaviour

The biggest variable — and the one you control

Module 3 · Your role

The CCS is executed at the filling line — by you

  • Gowning qualification is not a one-time event — it is a continuous demonstration of competence
  • Environmental monitoring reflects your behaviour in the room, not just HVAC performance
  • Intervention technique determines whether first air is compromised
Remember The CCS is not a document owned by QA. It is a set of behaviours performed by the operator.
Module 4 · Aseptic technique

Five principles you are tested on

  1. 1
    Never allow anything non-sterile to contact a sterile surface
  2. 2
    Protect first air — never interrupt the path from filter to critical zone
  3. 3
    Move slowly and deliberately — rapid movement disrupts airflow
  4. 4
    Minimise interventions — plan every action before entering Grade A
  5. 5
    Maintain spatial awareness — know where hands, sleeves and tools are
Module 4 · The first and last barrier

Gowning is a microbiological barrier

  • A gap at the glove–sleeve interface is a direct contamination pathway
  • A mask that does not seal releases respiratory droplets into Grade A
  • A hood that exposes hair sheds skin and hair particles continuously
  • Gowning certification must be current before you enter the aseptic core
Self-check before every entry Gown intact? Gloves secure? Mask seated? Jewellery and cosmetics removed?
Module 5 · Aseptic Process Simulation

What APS is — and what it is not

APS is…

  • A process simulation using growth medium instead of product
  • Executed with the same equipment, procedures, environment and personnel
  • Incubated to detect contamination introduced during the process
  • Equal to, or a worst case of, routine aseptic operation

APS is not…

  • A substitute for environmental monitoring
  • A one-off qualification exercise
  • Something you can "pass" with extra precautions
Critical If additional precautions are taken only for the media fill, the purpose of the APS is defeated.
Module 5 · Regulatory framework

The rules that govern APS

RequirementExpectation
Initial qualificationMinimum of three successful consecutive APS runs
RequalificationSemi-annual per line; each operator qualified at least annually
IncubationMinimum 14 days, dual temperature (20–25 °C then 30–35 °C)
Acceptance criterionZero contaminated units
InspectionBy personnel trained and qualified to detect contamination
Module 5 · Worst-case design

What the APS protocol is built around

Interventions

Type, frequency, duration, containers removed

Fill volume & container

Worst-case volume and line speed

Run duration

Number of shifts, including shift changeover

Personnel

Maximum number in the aseptic core

Hold times

Maximum exposure time for open containers

Environment

Representative of routine operation

Operator implication The protocol is designed around your routine interventions. Perform them differently and the simulation is invalid.
Module 6 · Interventions

Three types — and all of them must be simulated

Inherent

Planned activities in every run.

  • Filtration and filling
  • Lyophiliser loading / unloading
  • Routine sample collection

Simulated at the same frequency and duration as production.

Corrective

Well-understood operations to correct or adjust the process.

  • Container breakage
  • Stopper jam
  • Toppled vials
  • Equipment adjustment

Unplanned in production — but deliberately included in the APS.

Critical

Occurs within the aseptic processing area where product and critical surfaces are exposed to the environment.

Highest contamination risk — maximum first-air protection required.

Regulatory expectation 100 % of all non-routine interventions must be simulated — for type, frequency and duration.
Module 6 · Operator accountability

Interventions are recorded — and attributed

  • Every intervention during the APS is recorded with start and end time and the persons involved
  • A new corrective intervention not previously in the APS programme must be evaluated before batch disposition
  • Interventions performed in production that were not simulated are a direct observation and will trigger regulatory action
Common audit failure Interventions observed in manufacturing that were simulated at a lower frequency or shorter duration than actually performed.
Module 7 · Before the APS

Prepare — don't improvise

  • Confirm your gowning qualification is current
  • Review the protocol — especially the intervention list you must perform
  • Confirm you are fit to enter: no illness, no open wounds
  • Remove all cosmetics, perfume and jewellery
  • Rehearse the interventions mentally before you enter
Module 7 · During the APS

Simulate reality — nothing more, nothing less

  • Perform interventions exactly as in routine production
  • Do not add extra precautions or extra time
  • Announce and document each intervention as the protocol requires
  • Maintain first-air discipline — especially during corrective interventions
  • Do not try to "protect" the APS — handle problems per procedure
  • If unsure, stop and ask the supervisor
Module 7 · After the APS

The run isn't over when the line stops

  • All filled units are incubated without unnecessary delay
  • Do not remove or manipulate incubated units
  • If you inspect units, you must be trained and qualified for contamination detection
  • Any deviation during the run must be documented and assessed
Reminder An undocumented deviation during APS is itself a failure of the simulation.
Module 8 · Acceptance criteria

The only acceptable result

ZERO

contaminated units

Any contaminated unit is a failed APS. For fills of fewer than 5,000 units, even a single contaminated unit requires revalidation.

Module 8 · Consequences

What one contaminated unit triggers

  1. 1
    Root cause investigation to determine the most probable cause
  2. 2
    Corrective and preventive actions (CAPA)
  3. 3
    Three consecutive successful APS runs before the line returns to control
  4. 4
    Retrospective review and quarantine of all batches since the last successful APS
  5. 5
    If operator-related: restriction of duties until retrained and requalified
Module 8 · Failure modes

How APS runs fail

Failure modeTypical root cause
Operator-linkedPoor technique during intervention, first-air breach, gowning failure
Inadequate simulationAPS not representative of routine worst-case conditions
Environmental excursionHVAC or facility issue not detected by EM
Media / incubationGrowth promotion failure, incorrect incubation conditions
Invalidation (not failure)Mechanical failure, protocol deviation, power loss
Note An APS may be invalidated — but invalidation requires investigation and documentation. It is not a free retest.
Module 9 · Putting it together

Your personal contamination control checklist

  • My gowning is intact, sealed and qualified
  • All jewellery, cosmetics and personal items removed
  • I know the interventions I must perform — and have rehearsed them
  • I am moving slowly and deliberately
  • I am protecting first air during every manipulation
  • I am performing interventions exactly as in routine production
  • If something goes wrong, I follow the procedure and document it
